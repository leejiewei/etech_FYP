Privacy Notice

Last updated 22 September 2026

This site is a student final-year project. eTech Computer Accessories is being built and tested as university coursework. No order is really fulfilled, and during the test period you are asked not to send any money — the payment details shown at the checkout are placeholders and are not a real account. During the test period the site records how it is used, and this page explains exactly what that means.

Who is responsible

This site is operated by a student at a Malaysian university as part of a final-year project, supervised by an academic supervisor. Questions about anything on this page, including a request to see or delete your data, can be sent through the Contact page.

What is recorded, and why

If you create an account, we store your name, email address and phone number. These are needed to hold your basket, show your order history, and tell you when an order is ready. Your profile also has an address box, which is optional — it saves typing if you choose delivery, and you can leave it empty and collect instead. Your password is held by Supabase Auth as a hash — the shop never sees or stores the password itself.

Whether or not you sign in, the site records:

  • which products you open, and when;
  • what you type into the search box, and how many results came back;
  • what you ask the chatbot, and what it answered;
  • what you add to your basket, wishlist, and what you order.

This is the point of the project rather than a side effect. The research question is whether a shop's own records can be used to recommend products and forecast stock, so the browsing record is the subject being studied. It is used to show the shop's staff which products customers are interested in, to decide who is told when one of those products goes on offer (see Messages we send you below), to suggest items on the home page, and — in aggregate, with no names attached — as the dataset analysed in the written dissertation.

Being recognised without being identified

If you browse without signing in, your device is given a random identifier (a UUID) that is stored in this browser and sent with the records above. It is not a login, it proves nothing, and it is not linked to your name, email or any other person — it only answers the question "was this the same browser?", so that a single visit can be read as one visit rather than as a heap of unrelated clicks.

If you later create an account or sign in, the activity recorded under that identifier on this device is attached to your account, so that a purchase can be read together with the browsing that led to it. Clearing your browser's site data ends the old identifier and starts a new one.

If you place an order

An order records the items, the total, and the name and phone number you type at checkout. That is kept for as long as the order record exists, because it is the order.

An address is recorded only if you choose delivery. If you choose to collect, no address is asked for, and any address your browser happens to send with the order is discarded before it is stored — the phone number is how we tell you it is ready.

There is no card payment on this site and no payment gateway. Instead the checkout shows bank details and asks you to upload a photograph of the transfer receipt, which a member of staff then checks. If you upload one, we store that image. A receipt normally shows a name, an account or reference number and an amount, so please treat it the way you would treat any bank document.

  • Receipt images are stored in a private area. They are readable only by you and by the shop's staff — not by other customers, and not by anybody without an account.
  • They are not part of the research dataset and are not used in the dissertation.
  • You can ask for yours to be deleted through the Contact page.
  • During the test period there is nothing to pay, so there should be no receipt to send. Please do not upload a real bank document unless you actually intend to.

Messages we send you

If you have an account, the shop sends you short messages. They appear under the bell on this site and in the app, and — if you use the app and allow it to send notifications — on your phone as well. There are two kinds:

  • Order updates: when your payment is approved or rejected, and when your order is ready to collect or has been sent.
  • Offers on things you showed interest in: when the shop puts a product on a timed deal, a message goes to signed-in customers who saved it to their wishlist, added it to their basket without buying it, or opened it three or more times. Who receives it is worked out automatically from the browsing record described above; staff choose which product goes on offer, not who is told. If you browse without signing in, you are never sent one.

For every message we record when it was sent and whether, and when, you opened it. For offer messages we also look at whether you viewed or bought that product in the seven days afterwards. This is part of the research: it measures whether offers chosen from browsing are of any use to the people who get them, and it appears in the dissertation only as totals, with no names attached.

You can turn phone notifications off in your phone's settings at any time; the messages still wait under the bell. If you would rather not receive offer messages at all, say so through the Contact page and they will stop.

If we contact you on WhatsApp

The phone number you give at checkout may be used to message you on WhatsApp about that order — usually to confirm we have received your payment, or to ask for a clearer receipt. A member of staff writes and sends those messages; nothing is sent automatically.

WhatsApp is run by Meta, not by us. Anything you send or receive there goes through their service under their privacy policy, and a conversation stays on their servers and on your phone after the order is finished. If you would rather we did not use WhatsApp, say so and we will use email instead. You never have to start a WhatsApp conversation to buy anything — the receipt upload on the site does the same job.

If you submit a build to the showcase

The Rigs page lets you send in photographs of a computer you have built, with the parts tagged. If you do, those photographs and your first name are published on that page once a member of staff has approved them. Submitting is entirely optional, and you can ask for a submission to be removed at any time.

What is not recorded

  • No card, bank or payment credentials. There is no card box on this site and no payment is processed here.
  • No advertising or third-party analytics trackers, and nothing is sold or passed to advertisers.
  • No cookies for tracking. The site keeps your sign-in token and the device identifier in your browser's own local storage.

Where it is kept, and who can see it

Everything is stored in a Supabase (PostgreSQL) database. Access is restricted by database policies: you can read your own account and your own orders and nothing else, and the browsing records can be read only by shop staff. Questions sent to the AI assistant are passed to Google's Gemini API to generate a reply; the shop's product catalogue and your message are sent, and your name, email and account details are not.

How long it is kept

Account and order records are kept while the account exists. Browsing, search and chatbot records are kept for the duration of the project and the assessment that follows it, after which the project database is deleted. Data that appears in the dissertation appears only in aggregate, with no individual identified.

Your choices

  • You do not have to take part. You can browse without creating an account, and you can stop at any time.
  • You can ask for a copy of what is held about you, or ask for it to be corrected or deleted, through the Contact page. Deleting your account removes your personal details; the browsing records that remain are detached from you and cannot be traced back.
  • You can clear the device identifier at any time by clearing this site's data in your browser settings.

This notice is provided in line with the Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Malaysia). If you are taking part in the project's test period, this page is also the participant information sheet for that test.
